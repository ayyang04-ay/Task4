# Style analysis - RETRACTED

Two earlier versions of this file are wrong and are withdrawn.

Version 1 blamed the three-bullet architecture. That architecture is the writer's, not Claude's.

Version 2 blamed a set of collocations - "compensatory studies", "performs the same omission", "remark on",
"ambiguous to this point", "committed the following errors", "is the closest to addressing", "open-ended",
"fair/uniform" - and claimed they entered with Claude's text and survived the writer's rewording. **Every one
of those phrases appears zero times in any version of Claude's draft.** They are the writer's own words. The
claim was false, and it was the item the file called most diagnostic and highest leverage.

The wider list in version 2 (triadic lists, "rather than" as universal contrast, a single reporting verb,
register shifts, nominalisations, noun stacks) is also withdrawn as evidence of machine authorship. Those
features occur in LLM output and in competent human technical writing alike. Reading cannot separate "this is
machine-written" from "this is how this person writes", and the file asserted that it could.

## What is actually established

Verbatim overlap with Claude's drafts, measured rather than judged: 115 of 462 words, 25%, in 15 matched runs,
12 of them six words or longer, including three runs of 11 words. Spans are listed in overlap_report.md and
marked in place in overlap_annotated.txt. This is worth correcting because it is checkable.

## What is not established

Why a detector flags the text. The writer's register - structured, parallel, formal, with hedged doublets - is
a known false-positive class for these tools. Two rewording passes moved overlap from 36% to 25% without
changing the verdict, which is weak evidence that the remaining signal is not phrasing the writer controls.
Rewriting a natural voice to satisfy an unseen classifier is not a target this analysis can aim at.
